Identify the right person first · 50 sec

A venture capitalist or a founder?

He has experience in both, but began as a founder. This research concerns Michael Yang, the Korean American co-founder of mySimon and Become. He is a different person from Michael Yang, the former OMERS Ventures investor. A name-only search can mix their portfolios and views, so this profile uses mySimon · Become · Michael M. Yangas its identifying references.[01][02]

Born in Seoul in 1961, he moved to the United States at 14. He earned a bachelor’s degree in electrical engineering and computer science and an MBA at UC Berkeley, and a master’s in computer science at Columbia. His background spans technology, management, and marketing. Mucker’s mentor profile and Hanmi’s announcement list experience at Xerox, Intergraph, and Samsung.[01][03]

03

Understand the business to understand the investor · 1 min 20 sec

mySimon reduced the friction of finding information.

The idea came from an everyday frustration. In a 2026 interview, he described buying Microsoft FrontPage for $180, then seeing the same product for $160 at another nearby store the next day. That led to a question: “Could shoppers compare prices from different sellers in one place before buying?”That question became the starting point.[05]

An institutional profile says mySimon raised about $30 million in venture funding. This was company capital to grow the business, distinct from the founder’s personal income or the acquisition price.[01]

The customer’s goal was not to use a search engine, but to save time and money.As online sellers multiplied, finding and comparing products became more work. mySimon searched stores on the user’s behalf and gathered information to support a purchase decision. The diagram below explains that value chain; it is not a blueprint of the actual system.

More than a technology story

He emphasizes finding a technical co-founder, bringing in a designer, and building quickly, especially with complementary skills. Understanding technology did not mean implementing everything himself.[05]

Brand and distribution are part of the product

In a recent interview, he highlighted cost-effective PR, a recognizable brand and name, and unit economics: the economics of each customer or transaction. The advice extends from good technology to how it reaches customers.[05]

The key number in this research · 1 min 30 sec

A $700M acquisition ≠ $700M in personal wealth.

CNET acquired mySimon in 2000. The widely reported deal value was about $700 million.The January 2000 announcement based that value on about 11.3 million CNET shares and the previous day’s closing price.[18] The closing filing gives a different figure. CNET completed the deal on February 29, 2000, issuing 11,090,655 sharesin exchange for mySimon’s capital stock and options. The attached announcement valued the transaction at about $736 millionusing that day’s closing price.[06]

The difference reflects the valuation date of a stock-funded transaction.The reported deal value, the shares’ value at closing, and the founder’s eventual wealth answer different questions. Use the buttons below to explore each.

In a 2012 Yonhap interview, he said the CNET shares received in the exchange fell sharply after the dot-com crash, and that taxes and donations also reduced his wealth below what people imagined. The public sources reviewed here do not allow an exact calculation of his net worth or final proceeds.[04]

The sale also involved competing priorities. He recalls wanting an IPO and a larger company, while investors preferred an attractive acquisition offer. Founders may prioritize a long-term vision while investors prioritize an exit. A successful sale does not mean everyone shared the same goal.[04]

A principle for reading the numbers

His entrepreneurial achievement is clear. However, a major exit headline does not establish personal wealth, investment returns, or current investment capacity. This report includes neither unverified net-worth estimates nor fund-return charts.

05

After the first success · 55 sec

Become: tackling the problem again.

He continued building after mySimon. In 2004, he co-founded Become.comand served as CEO until 2012. It also combined shopping and search. This makes a one-off stroke of dot-com luck an incomplete account of his career. It does not establish that both ventures achieved the same scale of success.[01][07]

Become’s later transactions require care about dates and scope. In December 2014, TechCrunch reported that Connexity, formerly Shopzilla, had signed an agreement to acquire Become, with completion expected by year-end. The price was undisclosed. The founder’s LinkedIn account dates the US and European business sale to early 2015. No separate filing confirming the exact completion date was obtained for this research.[14] The first company’s $700 million figure must not be transferred to Become. Distinguish an acquisition announcement from completion, and the US and European businesses from the Japanese business.[07]

There were setbacks too. In a 2012 interview, he said ventures including NetGeo and Dreamlot had not succeeded as he hoped. This establishes his own assessment, not verified losses, investor harm, or the details of a wind-down.[04]

Japan was a separate transaction · Official filing

Metaps acquired Japan’s Become for ¥320M in 2016

Metaps’ June 28, 2016 filing announced an agreement to acquire 100% of the Japanese Become company from Become Japan Holdings for ¥320 million.The planned share-transfer date was June 30. A later official company history confirms that it joined the group in June 2016. Describing the Japanese business as part of the Connexity sale would therefore be inaccurate.[16][17]

The filing lists Michael Yang’s stake in the selling holding company as 39.3%. Multiplying that percentage by the acquisition price does not establish his take-home proceeds. The holding company’s debts, costs, taxes, and distributions are unavailable. A company transaction and an individual’s actual return remain separate questions.

Documented experience

A repeat company builder

His experience spans ideas, technical co-founders, fundraising, operations, and subsequent strategy. That gives him practical operating experience to draw on when advising founders.

Researcher’s interpretation

Learning matters more than the success story

Complementary teams, investor incentives, and trying again after failure are recurring themes. They do not mean every investment decision succeeds.

Three meanings of investing · 1 min 25 sec

What did he invest in, and how?

The word “investor” can blur distinct activities. His public record calls for separating angel investing in early companies, a private investment company, management of listed technology stocks.Backing a founder and buying a listed technology company differ in their targets, evaluation, and paths to realizing returns.

Documented in 2014
Angel

Participated in an early Korbit round

In January 2014, TechCrunch named mySimon co-founder Michael Yang among the angel investors in a $400,000 round for Korean bitcoin exchange Korbit. That was the total round, not his personal contribution.

Korbit: a named early investment

In contemporaneous VentureBeat reporting, he described bitcoin as a possible new growth engine for Korea, citing Korbit’s technical capabilities and customer loyalty. Reports on the same round also named Tim Draper, Naval Ravikant, David Lee, and Barry Silbert. This establishes participation in the same round, not personal closeness or an ability to make introductions today.[08] He saw potential in connecting a new technology with the Korean market. This was a particular investment judgment in 2014,not a statement that he invests on the same thesis today.[09]

Capital Management: a different activity from venture capital

A 2017 UCI event profile introduced him as founder and CEO of Michael Yang Capital Management. Later Hanmi filings describe investments in public equitiesof disruptive technology companies. It is more accurate to describe this as technology-stock investment management than as a conventional startup VC fund.[10][13]

The term “hedge fund” alone does not establish short-selling exposure, leverage, or hedging strategy. These sources describe an investment focus, not detailed rules or audited returns. A complete portfolio, assets under management (AUM), deal-level returns, and current check sizes were not verified.

What evidence of investment performance is available?

His participation in Korbit is documented; his individual return is not. A portfolio company’s later growth or sale does not reveal an early investor’s actual proceeds or return multiple.

How he thinks, in his own accounts · 1 min 10 sec

Technology is a starting point for him, not a conclusion.

Four themes recur in public interviews. The points below summarize his advice to founders; they are not an official fund’s investment criteria or an internal operating manual. Expressing a view does not establish that it guided every investment.

1

Find an understandable problem within a major technology shift

As the internet grew, he saw a need for search and comparison. In a 2026 interview, he emphasized real problems, clear user benefits, and a defensible competitive advantage, or moat. The interpretation here is that the costs and frustrations a technology removes matter more than novelty alone.[05]

2

Think about a larger market from the start

In 2012, he advised Korean founders to target the US and global markets from the outset. He also identified language, cultural understanding, and limited networks as practical obstacles. The context includes the ability and connections to enter a market, beyond its size.[04]

3

Build a complementary team

Technical co-founders, design, hiring, and execution recur in his advice. Despite his own technical education and experience, he shared roles with an implementation partner at mySimon. One person need not hold every specialty.[05]

4

Connect the path to revenue with the organization’s direction

He emphasizes PR, brand, unit economics, and a shared organizational vision. The advice connects building a good product, reaching customers, and creating sustainable revenue.[05]

A caveat · Separate past statements from current views

In 2012, he distinguished the direction of technological change from market overheating. A correct belief in the internet’s long-term growth would not make every company valuation or investment term attractive at every moment. This historical statement is not presented as his current view of the 2026 AI market.[04]

Checking what is current · 1 min

“Investor” alone no longer describes him.

His public activity in 2026 foregrounds his identity as an author, adventurer, and speaker.His official website and CKA profile introduce the memoir Coming Alive on the Rideabout travel, immigration, and entrepreneurship. Its listed publication date is March 10, 2026. Recent interviews also devote considerable attention to life after success and motorcycle travel.[02][05][11]

This does not establish complete retirement. Even where official materials still call him an investor, current startup deal intake, continued fund management, and the scale of activity require separate confirmation. A past title should not be treated as current investment authority.

Final assessment · 50 sec

Look beyond reputation to the help he can offer.

The public evidence strongly supports
experience in building companies and entering markets.
It does not yet establish
current investment capacity or fund performance.

His potential value lies less in one large historical number than in experience forming a team with a technical co-founder, turning user problems into products, and connecting them with capital and markets.Mucker’s listing of him as a mentor fits that role. Mentorship or advice does not establish a role as a Mucker investment partner or fund manager, nor expertise in every field. This research did not establish a specialist investment record in robotics or Physical AI.[01]

For a potential investment or mentoring relationship, these questions may help. Researcher’s suggested questions These are proposed questions to address gaps in public information; his answers have not been obtained.

  1. What roles are you active in today?Clarify whether his current focus is angel investing, managing a firm, mentoring, or speaking.
  2. Are you making new investments? In what areas and at what scale?Ask about recent investments, geography, stage, decision authority, and typical check size.
  3. What problems could we work on beyond funding?Ask about specific help with US market entry, co-founders, hiring, product positioning, or introductions to later-stage investors.
  4. How would we align incentives and time horizons?As his mySimon account illustrates, a founder’s growth goals and an investor’s exit goals may differ.
An assessment in one sentence

Michael Yang is a Korean American founder turned investor whose record includes a major company exit, setbacks, further ventures, and several forms of technology investing. Assessing him as an investor today requires additional evidence of current activity and results.
